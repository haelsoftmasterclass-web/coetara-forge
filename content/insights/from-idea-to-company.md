"Venture building" is used loosely. Sometimes it means a studio that spins out companies. Sometimes it is a new label on an old programme. At Coetara Forge it has a specific meaning: **the discipline of turning validated opportunities into operating businesses.**

The important word is discipline. Venture building is a repeatable process with clear stages, and each stage has to produce evidence before the next one begins.

## The path from problem to investment

Most ventures that reach investment have travelled the same path, whether or not anyone designed it:

**Problem → Validation → Team → Product → Customers → Revenue → Investment**

Venture building makes that path deliberate.

### 1. Problem

Every company starts as an answer to a problem. The first job is to find problems that are real, painful and currently unsolved, and to describe them precisely enough that they can be tested.

### 2. Validation

A problem only counts once the people who have it confirm it. That means customer conversations, observation and evidence of what people already spend time or money on. Validation is where most ideas should be changed, and some should be stopped.

### 3. Team

A validated problem needs a team capable of solving it. Venture building puts together complementary capability: someone who can build the technology, someone who understands the customer and the market, and someone who can make things happen operationally.

### 4. Product

The team builds the minimum viable solution, which is the smallest product that can be put in front of a real customer and teach the team something.

### 5. Customers

The product meets the market. Users, pilots, letters of intent or early revenue show whether the solution works outside the team's assumptions.

### 6. Revenue

A business needs a credible way to make money independently of subsidy or goodwill. Pricing, sales and unit economics move from theory to practice.

### 7. Investment

Investment becomes a sensible conversation once the venture has evidence: a real problem, a working product, engaged customers and a team that has shown it can execute. Investment is an outcome to be earned, not a promise.

## What makes it different from "support"

Training programmes teach. Accelerators speed things up. Venture building takes responsibility for the outcome. That means:

- choosing which opportunities are worth building,
- bringing in the operators, customers and distribution a young venture lacks,
- reviewing evidence honestly at every stage, and
- being willing to stop, merge or redirect ventures when the evidence says so.

## The Forge principle

Every stage should make us know more than we knew before. If a week of work has not changed what we believe about the problem, the customer or the business, it was activity rather than progress.

That is what venture building means in practice: build, test, learn, repeat, until there is a company.
