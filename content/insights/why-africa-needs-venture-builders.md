An accelerator is designed to make an existing company move faster. It assumes the hard parts are already in place: a founding team, a product that works, a first set of customers and a reason to believe the market is real. The programme then compresses the next stage of growth into a few months.

That model has done a lot of good. It also has a blind spot. Much of Africa's potential has not yet become a company at all.

## Where the potential actually sits

Look at where capability lives today. It sits with engineers inside banks and telcos who understand payment rails better than most founders. It sits with researchers whose work has never left the lab. It sits with operators who know exactly why logistics breaks between a port and a market. It sits with product people, designers and domain experts who have never been asked to start something.

None of these people would pass an accelerator's front door. They have no company to accelerate.

## What a venture builder does differently

A venture builder starts earlier. Instead of selecting finished teams, it does the work of assembling them:

- **It starts with people and problems.** The first question is who is capable of building, and which problems are worth their time.
- **It forms teams deliberately.** Technical, commercial and domain capability are combined on purpose, not left to chance meetings.
- **It validates before it builds.** Customer discovery and market evidence come before code.
- **It provides the missing infrastructure.** Distribution, customers, operators and commercial validation are brought to the venture rather than expected of it.
- **It earns investment with evidence.** Capital is the result of what the venture has proved, not the starting point.

## Why this matters for Africa now

African markets have real problems, real demand and growing pools of technical talent. What is often missing is the execution layer that turns that potential into operating businesses. Founders may have ideas without systems. Institutions may hold intellectual property without the capability to commercialise it. Corporates may have innovation mandates without venture-building capacity.

Accelerators cannot close that gap on their own, because the gap sits before the point where they begin.

## The Forge view

Coetara Forge was built to work in that earlier space. Forge Launch starts with exceptional individuals and helps them form teams, validate problems and build companies. Forge Commercial starts with under-used technology and research and asks whether there is a venture inside it.

Both engines share one rule: evidence before assumptions. A venture moves forward because it has shown something new, not because a programme calendar says it is time.

Africa does not need fewer accelerators. It needs more of the work that happens before acceleration is possible.
